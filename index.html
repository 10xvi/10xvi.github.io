The Strategic Start

But we start where it matters most and is most underinvested: the home. This is where people spend the majority of their time, where the body's natural regeneration occurs, and where continuous engagement becomes practical. The home is underserved by precisely the kind of intelligence that could make the biggest difference.

Critical Zones

Within the home, we focus on critical zones: spaces rich in sensing opportunity like bathrooms (where metabolic data flows naturally), and spaces central to regeneration like bedrooms (where sleep—our deepest healing process—occurs). We can build sensor networks ourselves or collaborate with existing infrastructure providers.

Outward Expansion

From the home, we expand outward—to wearables that travel with you, to clinics that integrate our intelligence into professional care, to hospitals that benefit from continuous patient understanding, to wellness environments that amplify the body's capacity for renewal. The roadmap is comprehensive because vitality doesn't respect boundaries between environments. But we start where we can make the deepest impact with the least friction.

Macro Economics

The Civilizational Imperative

This isn't just a product. It's a civilizational upgrade.

The current healthcare system is reactive, extractive, and unsustainable. It waits for breakdown, intervenes expensively, and manages decline rather than preventing it. The economics are backwards: we spend the most on people in the last years of degraded life, while investing almost nothing in the maintenance of vital health across the lifespan.

10X inverts this. By preventing decline rather than managing disease, we create a net-negative cost structure—the system pays for itself through avoided burden. Every chronic disease prevented, every hospitalization avoided, every pharmaceutical dependency eliminated represents capital that can be reinvested in human vitality.

"Better Than Free"

Our aim is to return avoided healthcare costs to users as a health dividend. This isn't a luxury for the wealthy. It's an investment in human capital that should be universally accessible.

Addressing the Hard Questions

"Won't this cause overpopulation?"

Longevity increases population linearly, not exponentially. The problem isn't headcount—it's lifestyle sustainability. The upside is more realized human potential: scientists completing their work, artists achieving mastery, wisdom accumulated and shared.

"Death gives life meaning."

This is a coping strategy for inevitable decay. Meaning can be grounded in expanding agency and creativity rather than scarcity. We don't need the threat of ending to make experience precious; we need the space to make experience deep.

"Is this only for the rich?"

Not if we build it right. When avoiding disease costs less than managing it, the system generates resources. Our model returns this value to users, making vitality an investment that pays dividends, not a luxury.

"Are you trying to make people immortal?"

The goal isn't immortality in the same form. We envision graduation—a voluntary, aligned transition to new forms of existence and consciousness—as a choice, not a surrender forced by decay. Death should be a door you walk through, not a cliff you fall off. Transcendence should be elected, not imposed by biological failure. The point is agency: choosing when and how to transform rather than having transformation forced upon you by entropy.
