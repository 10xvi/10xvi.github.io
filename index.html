01What the 10X Means

Ten Times the Healthy Lifespan, and Control Over Form

The number in the name stands for two goals. One is about how long people stay well, and the other is about the form of the body itself.

Goal 1Lifespan
10X

Healthy Lifespan

The first goal is at least ten times as many years in good health as people have today. Those added years should be strong and clear-headed, and the period of decline at the end of life should shrink.

Goal 2Morphology
Form

Control Over Morphology

Morphology is the body's form: its shape, its structure, and the way its tissues grow, heal and hold their pattern. The paradigm aims to let people maintain and repair that form, and in time to shape it by choice.

02The Problem

How the Body Loses Vitality

The body declines along twelve well-described pathways, known as the hallmarks of aging. They range from damaged DNA and worn-down telomeres to exhausted stem cells and chronic inflammation, and together they drain vitality year after year.

Evolution tuned the body for reproduction. It had little reason to keep us well for long afterward, and the body we inherited shows it. This initiative sets out to change that.

Why the body declines

Aging Is a Coordination Failure

The twelve hallmarks are best read as signs of one underlying failure. A healthy body is a community of trillions of cells that stay in step with each other, each doing its part to build, maintain and repair. With age, the communication between those cells breaks down, and one of the hallmarks, altered intercellular communication, names this directly. Cells lose track of the larger plan, and the damage the hallmarks describe is what that loss of coordination looks like.

Why the method fits

Bioelectric Signals Carry the Plan

Much of that coordination runs on bioelectric signals, the patterns of voltage that cells use to share information. These signals carry the body's plan: what shape to hold, where each tissue belongs and when to repair. Light, sound, temperature, breath and gentle currents can all influence this signaling. Their effect on a problem like telomere attrition is indirect: they help restore the conversation between cells, and cells that are working together again are better able to maintain and repair themselves.

03The Key

Interactive Learning

What makes this possible is an artificial general intelligence that works with the body in a closed loop. It reads the body's state, works out what is driving it, chooses a response, acts, checks the result and learns from it, again and again.

  1. 01

    Sense

    Reads heart rate variability, temperature, sleep, breathing and metabolic markers continuously.

  2. 02

    Understand

    Updates its picture of what is driving your current state.

  3. 03

    Plan

    Chooses the gentlest response expected to help, inside fixed safety limits.

  4. 04

    Act

    Responds through light, sound, temperature, breath and gentle currents.

  5. 05

    Verify

    Measures how the body actually responded.

  6. 06

    Learn

    Adds the result to its Vital World Model, so the next response fits you better.

What It Learns

A Vital World Model

As it goes around the loop, the intelligence learns a Vital World Model: a model of a person's whole body that predicts how they will respond to each intervention.

The model is learned from many kinds of body data gathered over time, such as heart rhythm, temperature, sleep, breathing and metabolic signals. It learns how the whole body behaves directly from those measurements, without having to be built up piece by piece from molecules and cells.

Shared

A Shared Model

The shared model improves with each person who opts in, so what is learned from one body can help others.

Personal

A Model of Your Own

Each person also has a model of their own body, tuned to how they respond.

Safety

How It Stays Safe

  1. 01It acts only for the benefit of the person it is working with, and it learns from the results.
  2. 02It never experiments on anyone just to learn something.
  3. 03Every action stays inside fixed limits that the intelligence cannot change.
  4. 04The less sure it is about a response, the less it does.
04Paradigm

The Paradigm Shift

Traditional medicine treats the body as a machine to be repaired when it breaks. In this paradigm the body is a living intelligence that can take part in its own care, and medicine works alongside it every day.

Before

Engineering

Care comes in occasional visits, starts after something breaks, relies on population averages and repairs the body from the outside.

Now

Conversation

Care runs all the time, starts before problems appear, follows each person's own biology and helps the body repair itself.

The Medicine of Conversation

In practice, the intelligence stays in constant two-way contact with the body. It sends signals through light, sound, temperature, breath and gentle bioelectric currents. The body responds, and the intelligence measures that response and updates its understanding before acting again.

This is medicine as an ongoing conversation with the body, carried on at the level of cell signaling. It runs day and night, and it follows each person's own biology as that biology changes.

Transparency and Ownership

05How the Paradigm Unfolds

The Path

In its full form, the paradigm reaches every place where people live, work, heal and rest. Homes, wearables, clinics, hospitals and wellness centers can all be connected by the same learning loop.

  1. First

    Beginning at Home

    The home is the natural starting point. People spend most of their time there, the body does much of its repair during sleep, and continuous contact with the body is easy to keep up. It is also where health gets the least support today.

  2. Within the home

    Two Rooms First

    Within the home, two spaces matter most. The bathroom is rich in metabolic signals that can be read without any effort from the person. The bedroom is where sleep happens, and sleep is the body's deepest repair process. Sensors for these rooms can be built from scratch or added to fixtures people already have.

  3. Beyond the home

    Reaching Outward

    From the home, the same loop can extend to wearables that travel with a person, to clinics and hospitals that gain a continuous picture of each patient, and to wellness settings built for renewal. The path covers all of these because vitality does not stop at the edge of any one setting. Home comes first because that is where the loop can do the most good with the least disruption to daily life.

06The Economics

The Civilizational Imperative

Getting this right would change how a whole civilization cares for its health.

Healthcare today is reactive and expensive, and it cannot keep growing the way it has. It waits for something to break, steps in at great cost, and manages decline instead of preventing it. Most of the money goes to the last years of life, after health has already failed, and very little goes to keeping people well along the way.

The paradigm turns this around. If preventing decline costs less than managing it, the approach pays for itself. Money no longer spent on chronic disease, hospital stays and long-term prescriptions can go back into keeping people healthy.

A Health Dividend

“Better Than Free”

The money saved when disease is avoided should go back to the people whose health produced the savings, as a health dividend. Then vitality is something everyone can afford, and taking part costs less than nothing.

OutcomeVitality as a Right

This becomes possible once keeping people well costs less than treating them when they are sick.

07FAQ

Addressing the Hard Questions

Safety“Is it safe?”

It is built around four rules. It acts only for the benefit of the person it is working with, and it learns from the results of those actions. It never tries something on a person just to see what happens. Every action stays inside fixed limits that the intelligence cannot change, and the less sure it is about a response, the less it does.

Population“Won't this cause overpopulation?”

Longer lives make the population grow slowly and steadily, without the runaway growth people tend to imagine. The bigger question is whether we live sustainably, at any lifespan. And the gain is large. Scientists would get to finish their work, artists would reach mastery, and people would keep sharing what they have learned for far longer.

Meaning“Death gives life meaning.”

That belief has helped people accept a decline that, until now, no one could avoid. Meaning can also come from what people create and choose to do, and from having enough time to go deeply into it. Experience can be precious without the threat of its ending.

Access“Is this only for the rich?”

It should not be. When avoiding disease costs less than managing it, the savings can be returned to people as a health dividend. Vitality then becomes an investment that pays people back, and everyone can take part.

Choice“Are you trying to make people immortal?”

The goal is to give people a choice about how their lives continue. We imagine that a person could one day make a voluntary transition to new forms of existence and consciousness, which we call graduation, at a time of their own choosing. Today that change is forced on people when their bodies fail.
